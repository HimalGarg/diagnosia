Privacy notice

This describes how the Diagnosia software works today. A qualified adviser has not reviewed it, and it makes no claim of compliance with any law.

What we hold

Account details, patient records entered into Diagnosia, uploaded documents such as lab reports, medicines, consent records, and a log of who opened or changed a record.

Photos and audio

Skin lesion photos and prescription photos are not stored. Consultation audio is sent for transcription and not stored. Transcripts are deleted when a note is signed, unless the patient agreed to keep them.

Waiting-room interviews

In DiagnoFast, a patient's answers, transcript and report are kept for the doctor who sent the link, and for the care team if the link belongs to a patient record. Voice recordings are encrypted before they are written to disk, so a doctor can check an answer against the patient's own words. Past health records shown with an interview are made-up demo data.

Stored documents

Uploaded documents are kept so a result can be traced to its source. They are encrypted by the application before being written to disk. Other records in the database rely on the protections of the server they run on.

Outside services

With consent, consultation audio, transcripts, interview answers and recordings, prescription photos and some unreadable lab reports go to Groq, in the United States. If Groq fails and a Google Gemini key is set up, the same content goes to Google instead. Drug names, never patient details, may be sent to the US National Library of Medicine and openFDA. Agreements covering real patient data with these services have not been confirmed.

Your choices

Consent can be revoked in the patient record, and tools that depend on it stop at once. You can see who opened your record. Requests to correct or delete a whole record, and how long records are kept, are not yet automated.
