# Diagnosia: MVL '27 Qualifier Video (2 minutes)

Flow and script for the 2-minute video pitch. Numbers come from
[MVL27_PITCH_STATS.md](MVL27_PITCH_STATS.md).

**Deadline: 30 October 2026.** Judges watch only the first 2:00, so aim for a
final cut of **1:55**.

---

## The core idea

**The video is two minutes long. So is an average Indian doctor's
consultation.**

A stopwatch starts at 0:00 and stays in the corner of the screen for the whole
video. At the end it reaches 2:00 and the closing line lands: *that was the
whole consultation.* The judges feel how short two minutes is, because they
have just sat through one.

The story in one line: **India's doctors have two minutes per patient and spend
them on questions a machine could ask, so we built Diagnosia.**

---

## What the judges score, and where the video covers it

| MVL criterion | Where it's covered |
|---|---|

| Quality of reasoning | The 76% insight: history is the most valuable part of a diagnosis and the part being rushed |
| Commercial potential | Market & Business (1:20–1:42): 14 lakh doctors, 4 billion consultations, ABHA, Abridge, revenue plans |
| Initiative & validation | Stage (1:42–1:54): working prototypes + doctor conversations (**see the gap below**) |

Spoken words are in normal text. *[Stage directions are in italics.]*

### 1. Hook (0:00–0:10), Speaker A

*[A, close-up, direct to camera. A taps a stopwatch on their phone. A stopwatch
graphic appears in the top corner and keeps running for the whole video.]*

> **A:** This video is two minutes long. In India, that's about how long a
> patient gets with their doctor.

*[Caption: "~2 minutes: average consultation in India. BMJ Open, 2017"]*

---

### 2. Problem (0:10–0:35), Speaker B

*[Cut to the Problem slide. Each number appears as B says it.]*

> **B:** One government doctor serves about eleven thousand people. Rural
> health centres are missing eighty percent of their specialists.

*[Cut to waiting-room footage: a crowded queue. Over it, text cards with the
doctor's questions: "Kya bimari hai?" · "Kab se?" · "Pehle koi bimari?"]*

> **B:** So those two minutes go on the same basic questions while the queue
> waits outside. Yet the patient's history alone gives the diagnosis in
> seventy-six percent of cases.

*[Big text on a dark screen: "76%". Hold it for a beat.]*

> **B:** The most important part of medicine is the part we rush.

---

### 3. Solution (0:35–1:20), Speaker C

*[Cut to the Solution slide: the Diagnosia logo and four tool cards.]*

> **C:** So we're building Diagnosia: four AI tools that give doctors their
> time back.

*[DiagnoFast demo, about 18 seconds. A patient in a waiting room holds a phone
or tablet. The app speaks out loud: "Kya bimari ha-i?" (product audio, not a
team member's voice). The patient answers. On screen, the transcript appears,
the doctor's form fills itself in, an "ABHA records" panel loads, and a
one-page report appears on the doctor's screen.]*

> **C:** Our lead product is DiagnoFast. While the patient waits, it asks them
> in their own language, listens to their answer, fills the doctor's own
> custom form and pulls their past records from ABHA. The doctor starts with a
> one-page report.

*[Skin Cancer Detection demo, about 8 seconds. A screen recording of the

>99% early vs 36% late. American Cancer Society"]*

> **C:** Our skin cancer tool screens lesions for doctors before costly
> biopsies, because caught early, melanoma survival is over ninety-nine
> percent. Caught late, thirty-six.

*[Prescription Analyzer, about 5 seconds. A phone photographs a handwritten
prescription. "1-0-1" turns into "Morning: 1 · Night: 1", and a diet card slides
in. Caption: "Only 1 in 8 patients properly understood their prescription.
KEM Hospital study, Mumbai"]*

> **C:** Our prescription analyzer explains prescriptions in plain language.

*[OptiKinesis, about 6 seconds. A screen recording of the working app: a

> **C:** And OptiKinesis lets people who can't use their hands control a

## Word count and pacing

Natural speaking pace is about 140–150 words per minute. The script has about
**270 spoken words** once the validation finding is added. That's roughly 1:52
at a steady pace, leaving a few seconds for pauses and the closing beat.

| Section | Target time | Approx. words |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 10 s | 18 |
| 2. Problem | 25 s | 58 |
| 3. Solution | 45 s | 103 |
| 4. Market & Business | 22 s | 61 |
| 5. Stage & Validation | 12 s | ~25 (depends on the finding you add) |
| 6. Close | 6 s | 10 |

Time a read-through out loud with a stopwatch before filming. Some lines that
look short on paper take longer to say.

**If you still run long, cut in this order:**

1. The "Why now" Abridge line in section 4 (keep it on the slide instead).
2. "Rural health centres are missing eighty percent of their specialists" in
   section 2 (keep it on the Problem slide).
3. "The rails are built" in section 4.

Never cut the hook, the 76% line, the DiagnoFast demo or the validation line.

## The validation gap (most important before 30 Oct)

"Initiative & Validation" is one of the five scoring areas, and right now the
video has nothing real to say there except "the prototypes work". **Even small,
real evidence beats none.** In the next two days:

1. **Talk to 5–10 doctors** (family doctors, local clinics, doctors you know).
   Ask three questions:
   - How many patients do you see a day?
   - Roughly how much of each consultation goes on taking the history?
   - Would you pay a monthly fee for a tool that took the history before the
     patient came in? Roughly how much?
2. **Write down one strong, true finding** for section 5, e.g. *"8 of 10
   doctors we spoke to said they'd pilot DiagnoFast."* Use exact numbers; don't
   round up.
3. **Show doctor quotes as on-screen text, not video clips.** The rules say
   "no one outside the nominated team may present", so a doctor speaking on
   camera could break the rules.
4. If a doctor agrees to a pilot, say so. A signed letter of intent is even
   better.

## Honesty notes for the demo footage

- **DiagnoFast and the Prescription Analyzer aren't built yet.** Make a mock-up
  of the flow (Figma or a simple prototype) and show a small **"Prototype"**
  label on screen. Judges score evidence relative to your stage, and a mock-up
  presented as a working product could cost you credibility.
- **Skin Cancer Detection and OptiKinesis can be shown as real screen
  recordings**, which is your strongest evidence. Use a clear, ordinary lesion
  image from the dataset, not a real patient photo without consent.
- The DiagnoFast app's spoken question ("Kya bimari hai?") is product audio,
  which is fine. Every *pitch* line must be spoken by a team member in their
  own voice. **Don't use AI voice-over** for any spoken line.

## Production checklist

**Filming**
- [ ] Every team member appears on camera and speaks
- [ ] Film in landscape (16:9), with good light on faces and a quiet room
- [ ] Use a clip-on or phone microphone close to the speaker; audio matters more than video quality
- [ ] Film each section separately, with 2–3 takes, then edit together

**Screen recordings**
- [ ] Skin Cancer Detection: upload, then result (real app)
- [ ] OptiKinesis: head-controlled cursor and blink click (real app)
- [ ] DiagnoFast: mock-up flow, labelled "Prototype"
- [ ] Prescription Analyzer: mock-up, labelled "Prototype"

**Editing**
- [ ] Stopwatch graphic in the corner from 0:00 to 2:00
- [ ] **Burned-in subtitles** (judges may watch with the sound off)
- [ ] A source caption under every statistic
- [ ] Quiet background music, well under the voices
- [ ] Final length **≤ 1:58**

**Submission**
- [ ] Upload to Google Drive with access set to **"Anyone with the link"**
- [ ] Open the link in a private window to check it plays
- [ ] Paste the link into the form sent to your E-Cell's email
- [ ] Optional: attach the pitch deck
- [ ] Submit by **29 Oct**, a day before the deadline
